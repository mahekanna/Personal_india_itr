RSUs, dividends and foreign shares

Three separate taxable moments, and they are easy to run together. Vesting is salary. Dividends are income the day they are declared, reinvested or not. Selling is capital gains, on a 24-month clock — not the 12-month one that applies to Indian listed shares.

Vesting schedules

Describe a grant once and every tranche is generated from it. A four-year grant vesting quarterly is sixteen separate salary events, each at its own price, its own Rule 115 exchange rate and its own 24-month clock — which is a lot to type correctly sixteen times.

Tranches with an actual price go into the return. Tranches still to come are valued at your estimate and used only for the advance-tax plan.

Rule 115 does not let you choose. Every transaction converts at the SBI telegraphic transfer buying rate on the last day of the month before it — so a vest on 15 September uses the 31 August rate. The rates below are built-in approximations until you replace them.

Schedule FA — foreign assets

This is the part with a ₹10 lakh penalty attached Schedule FA runs on the calendar year {% if foreign.schedule_fa.calendar_year %} ({{ foreign.schedule_fa.period_start.strftime('%d %b %Y') }} to {{ foreign.schedule_fa.period_end.strftime('%d %b %Y') }}), {% endif %} not the financial year, and there is no minimum value. A resident and ordinarily resident must report every foreign asset held at any point in that year, whether or not it earned anything. Omitting one attracts a flat ₹10 lakh under sections 42 and 43 of the Black Money Act — charged on the non-disclosure, not on any tax avoided.

The registered address and the year's peak price are not in any broker export, so they have to be entered once per holding.

Foreign tax credit — Form 67

Rule 128(9) wants Form 67 on the portal before the return. Tribunals have held the requirement to be procedural, but CPC denies the credit first and makes you argue afterwards. File it first: e-File → Income Tax Forms → File Income Tax Forms → Form 67.
